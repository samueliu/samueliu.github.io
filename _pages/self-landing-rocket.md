This was a senior capstone project aimed to create a publically accessible  and educational testing bed for self-landing and self-stabilizing rocket controls. My role on the team was an all-encompassing role including detailed hardware, electrical, and software design. More detail, including a video, assembly guide, and open-source files are on [GitHub](https://github.com/samueliu/self-landing-rocket/).

The hardware design focused on ensuring robustness and fidelity while maintaining simplicity. Key components included a custom-built frame, drone-based propulsion, and a novel coaxial thrust-vector-control system.

<div style="text-align: center;">
  <img src="/images/tvc.png" alt="TVC" style="max-width: 90%; height: auto; border-radius: 10px;">
  <p><em>Figure showing the TVC of the main </em></p>
</div>

The electrical components required rigorous mechatronic analysis of multiple actuators and sensors, with accounts for clock speed, frequencies, and microcontroller integration, as well as size constraints.

The resulting product was a fully-functioning drone rocket, with ability to provide accurate sensor data and actuate with a high enough fidelity for control algorithms to function properly. The device was designed to be easily remakeable with detailed instructions and readily-available parts, and was presented at the Fall 2024 Capstone Design Expo at Georgia Tech.
