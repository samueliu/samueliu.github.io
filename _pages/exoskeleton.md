I spent over a year at this lab as an undergraduate research assistant, with projects ranging from data analysis to hardware design. Some work may be proprietary, but I can hopefully showcase some of the projects I worked on for the hip and ankle exoskeletons.

I took part in redesigning the struts, actuator housings, and electronic housings for a former generation of the hip exoskeleton. Below are some iterations of the design process for the struts that attach to the actuators and electronic control board. As of 2024 the design has likely been revamped and photos of the full suit are unavailable, but these iteration used corrugations and strong materials to improve stiffness and support for the thighs.

My work in the lab also involved ankle exoskeletons, in which we had human subjects perform in-the-loop optimization on their gait cycles. I was in charge of data collections and analysis for metabolics, motion capture, and electrmyography. 

<div style="text-align: center;">
  <img src="/images/subject.png" alt="Hip dual" style="max-width: 35%; height: auto; border-radius: 10px;">
  <p><em>Human volunteer subject for ankle exoskeleton optimization and data collection</em></p>
</div>

The subject's natural gait cycle, measured by the timing of the ground reaction forces and ankle angle, were optimized to match up with the torques applied by the exo.

<div style="text-align: center;">
  <img src="/images/torques.png" alt="Hip dual" style="max-width: 65%; height: auto; border-radius: 10px;">
  <p><em>Graph showing gait cycle and exoskeleton support</em></p>
</div>

I analyzed the effects of exoskeletons and optimizations at different walking speeds on various cost-of-transportation metrics, particularly with muscle activity per distance traveled (CMAPD). It was noted that slower walking speeds had significantly worse metrics when using exoskeletons, while exoskeleton increased preferred walking speed and matching/lowering cost-of-transportation at higher speeds.
